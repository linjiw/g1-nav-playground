    Meet our learned planner

    A flow-matching model learns to generate whole-body motion from motion examples paired with obstacle scenes. It repeatedly transforms noise into a short motion, conditioned on the robot's recent poses, an elevation map, and a local goal. The output describes the pelvis and all 29 joints.

    State + map + route goalLearned motionsGeometric selectionSONIC + physics

    What the model does. Every 0.16 seconds of simulated time it samples 16 one-second candidate motions. Their joint trajectories come from the network. This edition has no fixed arm presets, forced sideways turns, retrieved motion clips, gradient guidance, leg IK, or world-model pose edits.

    What surrounds it. Geometric routing supplies nearby goals. Clearance and progress checks select among the generated motions, requesting more samples or stop-conditioned motions when needed. A fallback can still miss the clearance margin. SONIC handles tracking; navigation is not learned end to end.

    Tracker. NVIDIA's SONIC whole-body tracker turns the chosen motion into joint targets for 29 motors, simulated in MuJoCo. The solid robot follows physical dynamics, so execution can differ from the plan.

    What you see. The solid robot is the simulated motion. Teal shows the chosen plan and steering point; white lines are other candidates, and red lines fall below the geometric clearance margin. Dashed lines show the route. Optional ghost poses show planner predictions. Predicted clearance does not guarantee collision-free physical execution.

    Every attempt counts. All eight original scenes are included, with one fixed-seed attempt per goal. Contacts, falls, and time limits remain visible. The shoulder gate tests whether the model can discover a suitable posture without the former sideways-turn rule. This small demo is a capability inspection, not a held-out success-rate benchmark.

    Recorded simulation. The browser replays recorded plans and physics at ½×, 1×, or 2×; it does not run the model. CPU planning ran with simulation time paused during inference. Click an amber ring to select a recorded goal.

    Live or recorded. When the live server is up, the robot runs on a lab workstation's CPU and everyone watching sees the same world; one person drives at a time. Planning on a CPU is slower than real time, so the robot moves in slow motion and the timeline replays any run at 1×. When the server is offline, the page replays recorded runs.
